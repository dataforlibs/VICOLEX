Subject Themes in Ukraine's History

How much publishing attention nineteen distinct DK508 (Ukraine — History) catalog subject topics — eighteen named, solid-color topics, down to a final "Other topics" remainder — have received, year by year from 1727 to 2027. Unlike the companion Russia/Poland river, this chart tracks when books were published about each topic, not when the underlying events happened — see the methodology note for why, and how that changes what the shape means.

What this measures: for each year, how many distinct records whose subject headings match one of eleven original headline themes — Politics and government, Foreign relations, Antiquities, Nationalism & independence movements, World War II, Revolution & Civil War (1917–21), Famine (Holodomor), OUN & UPA (nationalist underground), Ethnic relations, Political persecution & prisoners, Euromaidan Protests (2013–14) — plus seven more named topics added later (People, Historiography & historians, Intellectual & cultural life, Local & military history, Description & travel, Ethnic groups, Social & economic conditions) — were published that year (the same LC call-number year used throughout this project). All eighteen are solid-color bands, each its own hue. A final catch-all, Other topics (shown with a diagonal-hatch texture, not a color — see below), covers every remaining substantive heading that isn't a place, a cataloging form label, or a bare date. Together all nineteen bands cover — of this catalog's 8,446 records — up from just 3,398 (40%) when only the original seven headline themes are counted. A record can count under more than one band.

Why some bands are named and others stay in "Other": asked directly whether the chart's named themes were exhaustive, the honest answer was no — most of the catalog's real subject matter fell outside the original seven bands entirely. Asked to show that remainder as distinct topics rather than one undifferentiated lump, the biggest, most coherent clusters were broken out into their own seven named bands (People is the largest at 1,010 records — any heading naming a specific individual, e.g. "Bandera, Stepan, 1909–1959" — while the other six group closely related segments, e.g. Historiography & historians combines "Historiography," "Historians," and "History and criticism"). What's left after that — real, but each too small or too idiosyncratic on its own to justify a 19th named band — is Other topics.

How eighteen colors fit a palette that used to cap at eleven: the first version of this expansion gave the seven new bands a muted texture instead of a color, since this project's dark categorical palette topped out at eleven hues its own colorblind-separation validator would pass. Asked to use color instead, seven new hues were picked from the original eleven's biggest open gaps on the color wheel, and a solver was run over all 18 colors' pairwise colorblind-vision distances to find the one stacking order where every ADJACENT pair (the only pairs that actually touch in a stacked chart) clears the validator's target threshold — not just its lower floor. Result: every adjacent pair is more visually distinct than this project's own bar for a passing palette requires. The one exception is Other topics, which stays textured on purpose (see below) rather than claiming a 19th hue.

Why "Other topics" stays textured: a plain gray solid was tried first for it and failed both the chroma-floor check (it isn't a real hue) and the CVD-separation check against its stack neighbor — and giving the residual catch-all a real color would visually claim it's a coherent 19th topic, which it isn't. It keeps the diagonal-hatch texture that's a perceptual channel independent of hue and color-vision type; exact identification comes from the legend, the hover tooltip, or the direct right-edge label, never the pattern alone.

Excluded throughout: cataloging form subdivisions that rank high in the raw heading counts but describe what kind of book it is, not what it's about (Biography, Congresses, Pictorial works, Dictionaries, Catalogs, Guidebooks, and similar), geographic place headings (tracked instead by the companion map and river), and bare date/century fragments.

Why publication year, not event year: the companion Russia/Poland river plots each mention by the year its OWN subject-heading date subdivision covers (e.g. a heading dated "1917–1921" counts toward those exact years) — but topical headings like "Nationalism" or "Antiquities" carry no date of their own to borrow, and even the episode-named themes (Famine, Revolution, WWII) overwhelmingly appear in books published decades after that episode, about it, not from it: this catalog has no book physically published during the 1932–33 famine, for instance. So every band on this chart is dated the only way that's actually possible here — by when the book itself came out — which makes this a chart of historiography (when people wrote about a theme), not of the theme's own chronology. The flowing curve is a —-year centered moving average of the raw yearly counts, smoothed for legibility; hover the chart, or switch to the table, for the exact unsmoothed number behind any point.
